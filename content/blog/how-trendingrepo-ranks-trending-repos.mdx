Raw GitHub star counts are a popularity museum: they tell you what *was* big,
not what's *moving*. A repo with 80k stars that hasn't shipped in a year is
less interesting than a three-week-old project firing on Hacker News, Reddit
and X at once. So TrendingRepo ranks by **momentum**, not totals.

## The momentum score

Every tracked repo gets a 0-100 momentum score that combines:

- **Star velocity** over 24h / 7d / 30d windows (not cumulative stars).
- **Fork growth** and **contributor churn** — real building, not just bookmarking.
- **Commit freshness** and **release cadence** — is it alive?
- **Anti-spam dampening** — star bursts with no follow-through get discounted.

On top of that we layer **cross-source mention signals**: when a repo is being
discussed on Hacker News, Reddit, X, Bluesky, Product Hunt and Dev.to at the
same time, that cross-platform confirmation is a far stronger signal than a
spike on any single source.

## Cross-source beats single-source

One feed is weak; five feeds agreeing is a real trend. A repo that's only
trending on one platform is often noise — recycled content, a paid push, or a
single viral thread. We weight cross-source agreement heavily and flag repos
firing on three or more platforms as breakouts.
